# tetris-arena

Standalone spinoff of the Tetris game embedded in [saiushi](../saiushi) (the owner's personal site). Purpose: experiment with real-time 1v1 online Tetris without touching the main site. Scoped explicitly as a **personal test sandbox** — no accounts, no payments, minimal ops, optimize for fast iteration over production rigor. See [README.md](README.md) for setup steps (npm install, Supabase project + SQL, env vars).

## Status

- **Phase 1 (done):** ported the single-player game (Zen/Sandbox, 40 Lines, Blitz) out of saiushi as a standalone Next.js app. `TetrisGame.tsx`, `TitleScreen.tsx`, `TetrisApp.tsx`, `tetrisConstants.ts` are otherwise-unmodified copies of the saiushi originals — keep gameplay fixes portable between the two where reasonable. Dropped: Sanity CMS (background themes are a one-theme local stub in `UseBackgroundTheme.ts`), the modal wrapper, `framer-motion`, and everything non-Tetris (blog, fortune slip, audio player).
- **Phase 2 (done):** room/lobby + synchronized start over Supabase Realtime, plus a `'versus'` mode on `TetrisGame` (races to 40 lines like Sprint). Verified end-to-end against a live Supabase project (two-tab test: presence sync, ready-up, synchronized start).
- **Phase 3 (done):** garbage-line exchange. Clearing lines sends an attack (standard-ish table: Single/Double/Triple/Tetris → 0/1/2/4, T-Spin Single/Double/Triple → 2/4/6, +1 for back-to-back, plus a combo bonus table) to an opponent over the same room channel; your own attack cancels incoming garbage before it lands (netted at your next piece lock, not instantly). `useOnlineRoom()` was lifted from `OnlineLobby` up into `VersusApp` so the Realtime channel survives the lobby→match handoff instead of being torn down when `OnlineLobby` unmounts.
- **Phase 4 (done):** rematch (a "Rematch" button on the post-match screen resets the ready/start state and returns to the *same* still-connected room's lobby instead of tearing it down, so everyone just ready up again — see `resetMatchReady` in `useOnlineRoom.ts`); a shared seeded 7-bag RNG (mulberry32, seeded once per match by the host alongside `startAt`) so every player sees the identical piece sequence, live-verified by reading matching NEXT-queue colors out of two tabs' DOM; and Quick Play — a public matchmaking queue (`useQuickplay.ts`, channel `tetris-quickplay-lobby`) that auto-groups strangers without a shared code, using presence + a deterministic sort-and-group algorithm (no server, so no locking — see the accepted-race-condition note below) rather than negotiation.
- **N-player rooms + match rules (done):** `useOnlineRoom`'s presence/ready-up/broadcasts are generalized from "exactly one opponent" to a list (`opponents`, `readyGuestIds`, `MAX_ROOM_SIZE = 8`), and Quick Play groups up to 8 simultaneous searchers instead of just pairing two. The actual match logic is N-ready too, not just the lobby scaffolding: garbage attacks target a **random still-alive opponent** (`VersusApp`'s `handleAttack`, using `sendGarbage`'s `targetGuestId`), the win condition is **pure last-player-standing** (clearing lines no longer wins on its own — a match ends only when you top out, or every other opponent is gone), and opponent board previews (`MiniBoard`, one per opponent, broadcast once per piece lock) render however many opponents are in the room. `VersusApp` snapshots the room's roster (`matchOpponents`) the instant a match starts rather than reading live presence throughout, so someone's tab closing mid-match can't shift the win-condition denominator — it can still shift who counts as *eliminated*, though, see below.
- **Disconnects count as eliminations (done):** `useOnlineRoom`'s presence-sync handler diffs each update against the previous snapshot (`opponentsRef`) — anyone who silently drops off presence (tab closed, connection lost) without ever broadcasting `'eliminated'` gets added to `eliminatedGuestIds` anyway, the same set an explicit elimination populates. Closes what was previously an accepted gap: survivors' last-player-standing win condition no longer stalls forever against a player who just leaves.
- **Routing:** single player is `/`, Versus is its own `/versus` route (chosen explicitly over keeping it as view-state, so rooms are shareable as `/versus?code=XXXXX` links — the host's lobby screen has a "Copy Join Link" button for this). A persistent top nav (`NavBar.tsx`) has a "Play ▾" dropdown split into **Local** (`/?mode=standard|sprint|blitz`, deep-links straight into that mode) and **Online** (`/versus`) — modeled loosely on Jstris's nav, deliberately without the rest of Jstris's chrome (no Leaderboard/Maps/About pages, no login, no chat, no live-board grid) since that's out of scope for now.
- **Match stats, session-scoped (done):** live "Sent"/"Received" garbage-line counters in the versus HUD (`linesSent`/`linesReceived` state in `TetrisGame.tsx`, accumulated at the same `lockPiece` call sites as the garbage logic itself), and a "Wins this session" counter (`winCount` in `VersusApp.tsx` — survives rematches, resets on a full Quit) shown in `OnlineLobby`'s ready-up screen rather than on `TetrisGame`'s win/lose result screen, so it's visible right where you'd decide whether to keep playing. - **PPS/APM/attack-per-minute, live (done):** Pieces Per Second and Actions Per Minute in Sprint, Blitz, and Versus's HUD (Sandbox excluded — it's a goalless practice mode, these don't mean much there), plus Attack/Min in Versus. - **Co-op game mode, Phase A (done) — the third GameMode, exactly two players sharing one board:** each player controls their own independently-falling piece; both land on the same shared grid, score/level/lines are team-shared, and either player topping out ends it for both (no win/lose split). `GameMode` (`useOnlineRoom.ts`) gains `'coop'`, following the exact same host-only-setting pattern `gameMode`/`lives` already use — a `<option value="coop">Co-op</option>` in the lobby's Game Mode `<select>`, Lives hidden (its guard changed from `!== 'practice'` to `=== 'versus'`, since neither Practice nor Co-op has an elimination to spend lives against). The hard problem this mode raises — two independent browser clients both needing to mutate the *same* logical board — has no precedent in this codebase (every existing mode is client-authoritative over its *own* board only; CLAUDE.md's architecture notes have always said so). Rather than build a first-of-its-kind host-authoritative model (one client simulates everything, the other just renders what it's told — meaningfully more complex, and adds input latency for whichever side isn't authoritative), Phase A reuses the *existing* board-broadcast pipe (`onBoardUpdate`/`opponentBoards`, already firing on every lock plus every ~200ms for Versus/Practice previews) with an **adopt-not-merge** rule specific to co-op: the receiving client replaces its own `board.current`/`scoreRef`/`levelRef`/`linesRef` with whatever the partner's broadcast says, outright (a new `useEffect` watching `opponentBoards`, gated on a new `isCoop` derived const kept deliberately separate from `isSandboxRules`/`isMultiplayerRoom` — co-op's gravity climbs like Versus, and its partner is overlaid directly on this client's own canvas rather than shown as a side preview board, so it must *not* pick up `isMultiplayerRoom`'s other use sites, the 1-on-1/compact preview column). `BoardSnapshotPayload`/`sendBoardUpdate`/`opponentBoards` gained `score`/`level`/`lines` fields (populated unconditionally now — Versus/Practice just don't read them) so a client always adopts a consistent "vintage" of shared state, not a fresh board paired with stale numbers. The honest tradeoff, stated plainly rather than glossed over: if both players lock within the same ~200ms broadcast round-trip, one client's very recent lock can momentarily get overwritten by the other's slightly-stale snapshot — a brief flicker that self-corrects on the next exchange (every lock broadcasts immediately, not just the periodic tick). - **Fix: co-op's shared board was intermittently erasing placed pieces — the "adopt-not-merge" design from Phase A was wrong, not just an accepted edge case (done):** per direct report — "a lot of lag and some pieces placed just disappear and doesn't register on both boards." Phase A's board sync did `board.current = partner.board` on *every* incoming broadcast, including the periodic ~200ms position tick that fires continuously during play, not just on lock. That meant roughly 5 times a second in each direction, one client's entire board could get wholesale-replaced by whatever the partner's snapshot said — and if the partner's snapshot happened to predate this client's own most recent lock (a near-constant condition under real two-person play, not the rare simultaneous-lock case the original design doc called out), that lock vanished. The resulting flicker as boards repeatedly snapped between two different views is almost certainly what read as "lag" too, separate from any real network latency. Replaced wholesale-replace with **merge-only sync**: extracted `sweepLines(boardMatrix)` as a standalone pure function (mirroring the existing `merge()` helper) from `lockPiece`'s previously-inline line-clear loop, so the same clear-completed-rows logic can run on either client's board. `BoardSnapshotPayload` gained `lockedPieceMatrix`/`lockedPieceX`/`lockedPieceY`, populated *only* on the actual per-lock broadcast (captured from `player.current` after `merge()` but before `playerReset()` reassigns it) — the periodic tick leaves them undefined, so the receiving effect can tell "this is a new lock to merge" apart from "just a live-position update, don't touch the board." On receipt of an actual lock, the client runs `merge()` + `sweepLines()` against its *own* board using the partner's piece — never replaces the array. Merging is commutative: applying this client's own locks and the partner's in any arrival order converges to the identical board shape, so a broadcast that happens to be "behind" something else can no longer erase it. Score/level/lines switched from adopt-verbatim to `Math.max(mine, partner's)` — these only ever climb, so taking the max is always safe and self-correcting once all locks have propagated, without the receiver needing to duplicate the sender's T-spin/combo/back-to-back scoring nuances to independently re-derive a number it can just be told. - **Fix: the partner's piece never actually rendered in co-op — a stale-closure bug in `draw()`, not a data problem (done):** per direct report — "in my testing I dont see the other player's pieces falling on my board at all." The board-sync and score/level/lines merge (see the merge-only-sync fix above) were genuinely working — verified live with matching scores — so the data was arriving correctly; the bug was specifically in *rendering* it. Root cause: `draw()` is only ever reachable through the `requestAnimationFrame` chain kicked off by a mount-only `useEffect` (`useEffect(() => { playerReset(); requestRef.current = requestAnimationFrame(update); ... }, [])`, deps `[]`). Because `update`/`draw` are plain functions defined in the component's render body, that effect's closure over them — and everything they read from props/state — is captured exactly once, at mount, and every subsequent `requestAnimationFrame(update)` call inside `update()` itself reuses that same frozen closure indefinitely. `draw()`'s partner-piece-overlay code was reading `opponentBoards` (the prop) directly, so it was permanently stuck reading whatever `opponentBoards` happened to be on the component's very first render — normally empty, since no broadcast has arrived yet at mount — and never saw a single subsequent update no matter how much real data came in. This is the exact same trap this file already has a comment about elsewhere (`toggleZeroGravity`, called from the same mount-only effect's `handleKeyDown`, reads `zeroGravityRef` instead of the `zeroGravity` state var for identical reasons) — the fix follows that established pattern rather than inventing a new one: a `useRef` mirror (`opponentBoardsRef`), kept current by a real reactive `useEffect` (`useEffect(() => { opponentBoardsRef.current = opponentBoards; }, [opponentBoards])`), with `draw()` reading `opponentBoardsRef.current` instead of the prop. This bug was specific to *this* frozen-closure rendering path — the separate `isCoop` adopt-effect (the one that actually merges board/score/level/lines) was never affected, since it's a normal reactive effect with `opponentBoards` correctly in its own dependency array; that's exactly why the underlying data sync could be verified working live while the on-canvas overlay silently never updated. Also added, per direct request ("we can make the 2p block's and ghost transparent with the 2p to differentiate"): a **partner ghost piece** — computed with the identical collision-scan approach this client's own `getGhostY` already uses (walk `pos.y` down via `collide()` until it hits something, back up one row), just run against the partner's piece/position instead of `player.current`'s, which is valid because in co-op it's genuinely the same shared `board.current`. Reuses the existing `'ghost'` `drawMatrix` variant (transparent fill, thin outline) rather than inventing a new visual style — the distinguishing element is the "2P" tag on the solid piece, not a different ghost color, matching what was actually asked for (transparent block *and* ghost, told apart by the label). The "coordinator" for the next round is whichever waiting player has the earliest `joinedAt` among the *current waiting pool* — recomputed fresh on every presence sync rather than elected once (unlike `useOnlineRoom.ts`'s persistent host), because the coordinator's own proposed round always includes themselves, so the moment it fires they leave the waiting pool too and the role passes to whoever's next automatically, with nothing to hand off explicitly. Once ≥2 are waiting, that client starts a local `ARENA_START_DELAY_MS = 15s` settle timer (or fires immediately if the pool hits `ARENA_MAX_PLAYERS = 100` sooner), then broadcasts `round-start` with a snapshot of the waiting roster, a fresh `roundId`, `startAt`, and a shared RNG seed — the exact same shape `useOnlineRoom.ts`'s host-decides-start effect already uses, just auto-triggered instead of ready-gated. Multiple rounds can be in flight simultaneously for different, non-overlapping subsets of players — nothing here enforces "exactly one active round," each client's own match is independent regardless of what else is running. A late arrival mid-round simply isn't in that round's already-broadcast roster and stays `waiting`, automatically picked up by whichever round gets proposed next — no separate "spectate" state needed. Once a player's own match concludes (win via last-standing, or elimination via topout — arena always runs at the fixed default Lives=1, no lives UI/setting exists for it), `TetrisGame.tsx`'s result screen relabels its button "Back to Queue" for arena and auto-fires `onRematchMenu` after a 4s beat regardless of whether it's clicked (a new small effect, `arenaAutoReturnedRef`-guarded against double-firing) — `useArena.ts`'s `returnToWaiting` then tears down the pod channel and rejoins the waiting pool, ready for whichever round the (possibly new) coordinator proposes next. This is what makes it feel "continuously running": nobody waits on stragglers once they're personally out.

Root cause: the flyout panel was `position: absolute` nested *inside* `<nav>`, and `<nav>` has `overflowY: 'auto'` for its own scrollable row list. Per the CSS overflow spec, setting one axis (`overflow-y`) to a non-`visible` value while leaving the other (`overflow-x`) at its default `visible` doesn't actually leave `overflow-x` visible — the browser computes it as `auto` too, so *both* axes clip. The flyout, positioned at `left: 100%` to sit beside the sidebar, was extending past `<nav>`'s own right edge and getting silently clipped to a sliver — exactly what the screenshot showed. This is a general trap for any `position: absolute` child meant to escape its container's bounds: it doesn't matter that the child is *positioned* outside the box, `overflow` on an ancestor still clips based on that ancestor's own box, regardless of which descendant is doing the positioning. **Fix**: moved the flyout out of `<nav>` entirely — it's now a sibling of `<nav>` within `<aside>` (which has no overflow rule) and switched from `position: absolute` (relative to the Play row) to `position: fixed`, with its `top` measured via `getBoundingClientRect()` on the Play row (captured in a new `playRowRef`) at the moment the flyout opens. ## Architecture notes

- **Realtime, no rooms table.** `useOnlineRoom.ts` uses a Supabase Realtime channel named `tetris-room:<code>` purely for presence (who's in the room) and broadcast (ready/start events) — no Postgres table backs a room. Keeps setup to one SQL block (just `tetris_scores` for the leaderboard).
- **Client-authoritative.** Each client runs its own board locally; only high-level events (ready, start, garbage, eliminated, board-snapshot) cross the wire. No server-side validation — acceptable for a sandbox among friends, would need revisiting before anything adversarial.
- **Synchronized start without teaching TetrisGame about Supabase.** The lobby computes a shared `startAt` timestamp + RNG seed (host decides once everyone's ready, broadcasts both) and every client's `setTimeout` to *mount* `TetrisGame` at that instant. Since `TetrisGame`'s own internal 3-2-1-GO countdown then runs identically on all sides from the same starting instant, gameplay begins in lockstep. `TetrisGame.tsx` still has zero direct knowledge of Supabase/Realtime — its multiplayer surface is a handful of plain optional props (`onAttack`, `incomingGarbage`, `onEliminated`, `eliminatedOpponentIds`, `opponentCount`, `onRematchMenu`, `seed`, `onBoardUpdate`, `opponentBoards`) that `VersusApp` wires to `useOnlineRoom()`'s send functions/broadcast state (with `VersusApp` doing the random-target selection itself, per the note above), keeping the game component transport-agnostic and free of any room-topology awareness.
- **No server, so no locking — accepted for a friends-scale sandbox.** Both the room's ready-up and quickplay's matchmaking are computed independently by each client from its own (eventually-consistent) view of Realtime presence, not negotiated with a central authority. This is occasionally observable: e.g. if 3 people click Quick Play within the same instant, presence sync can momentarily disagree and one person can be left waiting alone rather than grouped in — recoverable by just searching again (confirmed live: this happened in testing, and the leftover player matched normally on a second attempt). Not worth a locking table for this project's scale.
- **Ephemeral identity.** A random guest ID per browser tab (`useOnlineRoom.ts`/`useQuickplay.ts`), nothing persisted, no login.
- **Known accepted lint noise:** this codebase carries a fixed baseline of experimental React-Compiler ESLint findings (`react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` etc.) inherited from the saiushi original — same 9 findings there, plus a few more added deliberately since: `useOnlineRoom.ts`'s host-decides-start effect, and the mount-only "read a client-only source (localStorage/URL), then setState once to sync it in" shape shared by `useSearchParam.ts` and `useColorTheme.ts` (both intentionally hydration-safe — state starts equal to the SSR default, then a mount effect reads the real client-side value; there's no way to do that read outside an effect). Consistent with the existing baseline's shape, not something worth restructuring for a sandbox hook. Don't chase these to zero; just don't let the count creep up for reasons unrelated to that pattern.
